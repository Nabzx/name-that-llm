Name That LLM07 / 10

What didn't work

Peer correction: 0.631 → 0.622, our worst probe.

The idea

A public trick: inside each topic cluster, remove the lean the SVC gives every passage, and drop class quotas.

Why we tried it

Its author saw +0.010, on a weaker blend. On paper it targets the same problem as our hypothesis about prompts.

Why it failed

With no quotas, a strong blend drifts towards the models it already favours. On the full public stack it also lost (−0.001).

Lesson: a trick that rescues a weak blend can hurt a strong one. Measure it on your own base.

Name That LLM08 / 10

What the leaderboard told us

Four ties at 0.631, then a jump to 0.637.

The public slice is small

Probes 3, 5, 7 and 8 each changed real members, and each scored 0.631. Gains of a few thousandths round away, so we steered by cross-validation, not the board.

The Gaussian head needed full weight

At 0.4 it moved too few rows. At 1.0, the weight our cross-validation picked, plus ModernBERT, the score rose to 0.637.

0.637

Best public score (probe 9), up from 0.626 at our first probe and 0.620 for the published spine alone.

What I'd try next

More data where we are weakest, and honest numbers for every member.

01

Bigger pool

Retrain on the open 122k pool. Stage 1 alone is about 10 h on two T4s.

02

Five folds each

Fold models for every member, so the blend is fitted on honest scores, not probes.

03

Joint decoding

One Gaussian group model across all members, with one shared prompt effect.

04

The hard pairs

Targeted data for the GLM and Gemini siblings, once the rules on generated data are clear.
